# Visual Language — Zombie Apocalypse Film

## Camera Philosophy
The camera is a survivor too. It has limited energy, no time for elegance, and it witnesses without editorializing. Motivated camera movement only — if the camera moves, something caused it to move.

**Primary mode:** Handheld, shoulder-mounted weight. Not shaky-cam chaos — controlled instability. The camera breathes.

**Secondary mode:** Locked-off static for emotional weight. When the camera finally goes still, the audience should feel it.

## Lensing
- **Focal lengths:** 35mm and 50mm dominant. Close enough to feel proximity, wide enough to keep environment in frame. Telephoto compression (85mm+) for surveillance-feeling observation shots — we're watching characters who don't know they're being watched.
- **Depth of field:** Shallow in intimate scenes (faces in focus, world falling away), deeper in exterior/threat scenes (nowhere to escape the environment).
- **No drone aerials** unless used for scale of devastation, sparingly. The film lives at ground level.

## Color & Grade
| Environment | Color Direction |
|-------------|----------------|
| Exterior day | Desaturated, slightly cool — color has drained from the world |
| Interior shelter | Warmer, amber/candlelight tones — warmth is earned and precious |
| Night/threat | Near-monochrome, available light only, deep shadows |
| Memory/flashback (if used) | Oversaturated by contrast — the past was vivid; the present is not |

**Overall:** Pull saturation globally. Let skin tones stay warm — humanity against a drained world.

## Lighting
**No power grid** — all lighting is motivated by source:
- Daylight through boarded windows (raking, dramatic)
- Candles, lanterns, firelight (warm, flickering, intimate)
- Flashlights (narrow, anxious, creates shadow threat)
- Fire (exterior threat/drama scenes)

## Composition
- **Doorframes and windows** as natural framing devices — survivors are always framed by thresholds they may or may not cross
- **Dead space** — leave emptiness in frame where people used to be
- **Crowd absence** — frame compositions that feel wrong because they're missing expected human density (empty parking lots, silent streets)
- **Horizon lines** — keep them slightly unstable in handheld work; stabilize only when characters have a moment of genuine clarity

## Editing Visual Rhythm (see editing-rhythm.md for full detail)
- No quick cuts during quiet scenes
- Action cut on movement, not on sound
- Let reaction shots breathe — three extra seconds of a face is worth more than coverage

## Studio Brain Application
Use this doc to evaluate scene descriptions and shot suggestions. Flag any visual suggestion that reads as stylized, slick, or action-genre. When advising on scene staging, reference specific lighting sources available per the world rules. Always ask: what is the camera's relationship to this moment?
