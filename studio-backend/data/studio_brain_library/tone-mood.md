# Tone & Mood — Zombie Apocalypse Film

## Emotional Register
The film lives in **exhausted grief**, not active terror. The horror of the zombie apocalypse isn't the monsters — it's the weight of everything already lost. Fear is a baseline condition, not a peak event. Jump scares are cheap here; dread is the currency.

The tone sits closest to:
- *The Road* (McCarthy) — bleak love, forward motion against annihilation
- *28 Days Later* — handheld intimacy, human threat as the real villain
- *Leave No Trace* — quiet survival competence, minimal dialogue, earned emotion

Avoid: campy humor, quip-heavy action beats, clean heroics. Every win costs something.

## Emotional Arc Across the Film
| Phase | Dominant Feeling |
|-------|-----------------|
| Opening | Disorientation, shock still present |
| Early middle | Hollow routine, numbness as coping |
| Crisis | Grief cracking through numbness |
| Climax | Desperate clarity — what actually matters |
| End | Ambiguous survival — relief shadowed by loss |

## What the Camera Feels
The camera is tired. It doesn't stylize violence. It witnesses. Static holds too long. Handheld has weight, not kinetic excitement — more *United 93* than *World War Z*.

## Humor (Rare, Earned)
Dark, dry, involuntary. A survivor laughing at something absurd because the alternative is crying. Never winking at the audience.

## Sound Mood
Silence is the most important sound element. The world is *quieter* than it should be. That wrongness should register before dialogue or score explains it.

## Studio Brain Application
When advising on scene tone, calibrate against exhausted grief as the baseline. Push back on anything that reads as triumphant or energetic without earning it. Flag moments where the script reaches for genre comfort over emotional honesty.
