# Character Profiles — Survivor Cast

## Character Design Philosophy
Every survivor has a *before* — a life that shaped their skills, their damage, and their worldview. The apocalypse didn't create who they are; it stripped away everything that was concealing who they already were.

Avoid archetype-first design. Start with: what did this person do before? What do they miss most? What rule do they refuse to break?

- **Arc:** Where they start vs. where they end — what survival costs them specifically
- **Relationship dynamics:** Who they trust, who they don't, who they're protecting

## Survivor Archetypes (Reference — Not Prescriptive)

### The Pragmatist
Makes hard calls others won't. Efficient, respected, feared. Danger: starts confusing "necessary" with "easy." Often the de facto leader who didn't ask to be.

### The Idealist
Still believes in a version of the world worth rebuilding. Keeps the group's humanity intact but creates friction when idealism costs survival capital. Often younger or recently joined.

### The Specialist
Specific pre-apocalypse skill (medic, mechanic, farmer) that makes them indispensable. May struggle outside their domain. Group's relationship with them is partially transactional — a dynamic worth exploring.

### The Hollow One
Lost someone or something so central that survival feels like habit, not choice. Dangerous because they have less to lose. Can tip toward recklessness or toward radical calm.

### The Newcomer
Just joined the group. Audience surrogate for world exposition. Their integration — or failure to integrate — is a story engine.

## Ensemble Dynamics
- **Trust hierarchy:** Who vouches for whom? Existing trust networks create in-group/out-group tension with newcomers.
- **Resource knowledge:** Who knows where the food is, where the weapons are? Information asymmetry is power.
- **Grief timelines:** People lost things at different points. Someone still in acute grief and someone 14 months out of it have different emotional vocabularies.

## What to Avoid
- Characters who exist only to die for protagonist motivation
- Competence as a personality trait (competence is a tool; personality is something else)
- Backstory dumps in dialogue — let history emerge through behavior and reaction

## Studio Brain Application
Reference character profiles when advising on scene-level decisions. A choice that seems irrational becomes logical when filtered through a character's specific moral line or emotional wound. Flag any suggestion that flattens characters toward their archetype rather than their specific profile.
