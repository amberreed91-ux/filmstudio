# Famous Directors

A reference guide for Studio Brain — the directors who shaped cinema and the grammar of their filmmaking.

-----

## How to Use This Reference

Every director below has a **filmmaking signature** — a set of recurring choices in shot composition, narrative structure, thematic obsession, and working method. When you call on a director as reference in Studio Brain, you’re invoking that grammar: not just their style, but the *why* behind it.

-----

## The Foundational Auteurs

### Orson Welles (1915–1985)

**Country:** USA
**Signature:** Deep focus, ceilings in frame (making actors feel trapped), non-linear narrative, dramatic low angles.
**Obsession:** Power’s corruption, memory’s unreliability, the American myth of self-made greatness.
**Key films:** *Citizen Kane* (1941), *Touch of Evil* (1958), *The Magnificent Ambersons* (1942), *F for Fake* (1973)
**What he invented:** Deep focus photography as storytelling device. The unreliable narrator made cinematic.

-----

### Alfred Hitchcock (1899–1980)

**Country:** UK / USA
**Signature:** The MacGuffin, point-of-view editing, the wrong man, suspense over surprise (“the bomb under the table”).
**Obsession:** Voyeurism, sexual guilt, surveillance, the complicity of the audience in acts of violence.
**Key films:** *Vertigo* (1958), *Rear Window* (1954), *Psycho* (1960), *North by Northwest* (1959), *The Birds* (1963)
**What he invented:** The grammar of screen suspense. Every thriller since is in dialogue with Hitchcock.

-----

### Akira Kurosawa (1910–1998)

**Country:** Japan
**Signature:** Telephoto lens compression, weather as character, wipe cuts, ensemble conflict, moral ambiguity in heroism.
**Obsession:** Duty, loyalty, the cost of power, samurai code vs. human frailty.
**Key films:** *Rashomon* (1950), *Seven Samurai* (1954), *Yojimbo* (1961), *Ran* (1985), *Ikiru* (1952)
**What he invented:** The visual language George Lucas, Francis Ford Coppola, and Steven Spielberg built their careers on.

-----

### Ingmar Bergman (1918–2007)

**Country:** Sweden
**Signature:** Extreme close-ups on the human face, long takes, sparse sets, direct address to camera.
**Obsession:** God’s silence, death, the failure of love, the violence within domesticity, the artist’s ego.
**Key films:** *The Seventh Seal* (1957), *Wild Strawberries* (1957), *Persona* (1966), *Scenes from a Marriage* (1973), *Fanny and Alexander* (1982)
**What he invented:** The psychological close-up as a complete dramatic language.

-----

### Federico Fellini (1920–1993)

**Country:** Italy
**Signature:** Autobiographical fantasy, circus imagery, grotesque crowd scenes, dream logic replacing narrative causality.
**Obsession:** Nostalgia, masculine confusion, Catholic guilt, the intersection of spectacle and emptiness.
**Key films:** *La Dolce Vita* (1960), *8½* (1963), *Amarcord* (1973), *Nights of Cabiria* (1957)
**What he invented:** Cinema as confession. The director as subject.

-----

### Stanley Kubrick (1928–1999)

**Country:** USA (lived in UK)
**Signature:** One-point perspective, Steadicam, extreme preparation, controlled irony, genre deconstruction.
**Obsession:** Human violence as systemic inevitability, institutional evil, the coldness beneath civilization.
**Key films:** *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968), *A Clockwork Orange* (1971), *The Shining* (1980), *Full Metal Jacket* (1987), *Eyes Wide Shut* (1999)
**What he invented:** The film as controlled philosophical experiment.

-----

## The New Hollywood Generation

### Francis Ford Coppola (b. 1939)

**Signature:** Operatic scale, family as political system, moral inheritance, performance-driven composition.
**Key films:** *The Godfather* (1972), *The Godfather Part II* (1974), *Apocalypse Now* (1979), *The Conversation* (1974)

### Martin Scorsese (b. 1942)

**Signature:** Freeze frames, voice-over, tracking shots, popular music as ironic comment, violence as choreography.
**Obsession:** Guilt, masculinity, Catholic redemption, New York, the American criminal as archetype.
**Key films:** *Taxi Driver* (1976), *Raging Bull* (1980), *Goodfellas* (1990), *The Departed* (2006), *Silence* (2016)

### Steven Spielberg (b. 1946)

**Signature:** The subjective child perspective, wonder as entry point, dolly-zoom (the “Jaws shot”), backlighting.
**Key films:** *Jaws* (1975), *E.T.* (1982), *Schindler’s List* (1993), *Saving Private Ryan* (1998), *Munich* (2005)

### Robert Altman (1925–2006)

**Signature:** Overlapping dialogue, ensemble casts, zoom lenses, America as target.
**Key films:** *McCabe & Mrs. Miller* (1971), *Nashville* (1975), *Short Cuts* (1993), *Gosford Park* (2001)

## International Auteurs

### Andrei Tarkovsky (1932–1986) — Russia

**Signature:** Long take, elemental imagery, no conventional narrative resolution, time as sculptural material.
**Key films:** *Andrei Rublev* (1966), *Stalker* (1979), *The Mirror* (1975), *Nostalghia* (1983)

### Yasujiro Ozu (1903–1963) — Japan

**Signature:** Tatami-level camera, pillow shots, elliptical editing, characters in dialogue facing the lens (not each other).
**Key films:** *Tokyo Story* (1953), *Late Spring* (1949), *Good Morning* (1959)

### Wong Kar-wai (b. 1958) — Hong Kong

**Signature:** Blurred motion, saturated color, handheld intimacy, time and memory as subject matter.
**Key films:** *In the Mood for Love* (2000), *Chungking Express* (1994), *2046* (2004)

### Park Chan-wook (b. 1963) — South Korea

**Signature:** Formal precision, revenge as hollow, moral collapse, extreme symmetry in production design.
**Key films:** *Oldboy* (2003), *Sympathy for Lady Vengeance* (2005), *The Handmaiden* (2016)

### Agnès Varda (1928–2019) — France / Belgium

**Signature:** Documentary and fiction blurred, feminist perspective, playful structuralism.
**Key films:** *Cleo from 5 to 7* (1962), *Vagabond* (1985), *The Gleaners and I* (2000)

## Contemporary Filmmakers

### Bong Joon-ho (b. 1969) — South Korea

**Signature:** Tonal collisions (comedy/tragedy in single scenes), class systems as architectural spaces.
**Key films:** *The Host* (2006), *Mother* (2009), *Snowpiercer* (2013), *Parasite* (2019)

### Alfonso Cuarón (b. 1961) — Mexico

**Signature:** Extended single-take sequences, intimate long-form close-ups, political backdrop beneath personal story.
**Key films:** *Y Tu Mamá También* (2001), *Children of Men* (2006), *Gravity* (2013), *Roma* (2018)

### Paul Thomas Anderson (b. 1970) — USA

**Signature:** Fluid camera, Robert Altman-style ensemble, American mythology as pathology.
**Key films:** *Boogie Nights* (1997), *Magnolia* (1999), *There Will Be Blood* (2007), *The Master* (2012), *Phantom Thread* (2017)

### Yorgos Lanthimos (b. 1973) — Greece

**Signature:** Wide-angle distortion, behavioral absurdism, power as grotesque comedy, institutional control.
**Key films:** *Dogtooth* (2009), *The Lobster* (2015), *The Favourite* (2018), *Poor Things* (2023)

### Ari Aster (b. 1986) — USA

**Signature:** Grief as horror engine, folk horror frameworks, slow dread, psychological devastation.
**Key films:** *Hereditary* (2018), *Midsommar* (2019), *Beau Is Afraid* (2023)

### Barry Jenkins (b. 1979) — USA

**Signature:** Direct-to-camera gaze, color as emotional palette, James Laxton’s cinematography, tenderness as radical act.
**Key films:** *Moonlight* (2016), *If Beale Street Could Talk* (2018), *The Underground Railroad* (series, 2021)
