# A24 Filmmaking

A reference guide for Studio Brain — the aesthetic, philosophy, and grammar of A24’s creative identity.

-----

## What Is A24?

A24 is an American independent film and television production and distribution company founded in 2012. In roughly a decade, it became the most culturally significant indie studio in American cinema — not through budget, but through a consistent aesthetic identity and willingness to bet on singular directorial visions. A24 films don’t look like each other, but they feel like each other.

Their model: low-to-mid budgets, director-driven projects, prestige marketing, cultural tastemaker positioning. The A24 brand became the film itself — audiences seek out A24 releases the way they once sought out specific directors.

-----

## The A24 Aesthetic: What It Actually Means

The “A24 aesthetic” is sometimes used lazily (pastel color grading, mumblecore dialogue, quirky melancholy). The deeper truth is more specific: **A24 consistently chooses films that treat genre conventions as emotional vehicles rather than entertainment formulas.**

### Core Characteristics

**1. Genre Deconstruction**
A24 films typically take a recognizable genre — horror, coming-of-age, family drama, thriller — and use its architecture to carry something the genre doesn’t usually carry. *Hereditary* is a grief film wearing horror’s skin. *Lady Bird* is a mother-daughter film wearing teen comedy’s skin. *Everything Everywhere All at Once* is a midlife crisis film wearing multiverse action’s skin.

**2. Emotional Specificity Over Spectacle**
A24 films trust that very specific, personal, particular emotional experiences will resonate universally — the opposite of the Hollywood instinct to sand every edge smooth. The uncomfortable, the strange, the specific — these are A24’s vocabulary.

**3. Director as Author**
A24 treats its directors as auteurs. Ari Aster. Greta Gerwig. Barry Jenkins. Sofia Coppola. Robert Eggers. Each film carries a distinctive personal vision rather than a house style imposed by the studio. The studio’s identity comes from *curating* auteurs, not overwriting them.

**4. Uncomfortable Thematic Territory**
Trauma, grief, family dysfunction, sexuality, identity, systemic oppression, death — treated with directness rather than sentiment. A24 doesn’t soften the hard thing. It leans into it.

**5. Tonal Hybridity**
The most A24 thing: holding comedy and tragedy in the same scene without resolving the tension between them. The absurd and the devastating cohabiting. *The Lobster*. *The Favourite*. *After Yang*. *Beef*.

## The A24 Film Catalog: Key Examples by Mode

### Elevated Horror

Horror that uses genre mechanics to explore psychological or emotional truth.

- *Hereditary* (2018, dir. Ari Aster) — grief as supernatural horror
- *Midsommar* (2019, dir. Ari Aster) — breakup and codependency in folk horror
- *The Witch* (2015, dir. Robert Eggers) — Puritan religious paranoia made literal
- *It Comes at Night* (2017, dir. Trey Edward Shults) — trust’s collapse in apocalypse
- *Talk to Me* (2022, dir. Danny and Michael Philippou) — grief and addiction as possession

### Quiet Character Study

Interior, slow-burn films about identity, relationships, and time.

- *Moonlight* (2016, dir. Barry Jenkins) — identity, sexuality, Black masculinity in three chapters
- *Lady Bird* (2017, dir. Greta Gerwig) — mother-daughter conflict and the ache of leaving home
- *After Yang* (2021, dir. Kogonada) — grief, memory, and what makes us human
- *C’mon C’mon* (2021, dir. Mike Mills) — found family, childhood, disconnection

### Genre Experimentation

Films that wear a genre’s structure while subverting its expectations.

- *Everything Everywhere All at Once* (2022, dir. Daniels) — multiverse action as immigrant family drama
- *Ex Machina* (2014, dir. Alex Garland) — sci-fi thriller as gender and power study
- *Under the Skin* (2013, dir. Jonathan Glazer) — alien horror as feminist body study
- *Good Time* (2017, dir. Safdie Brothers) — crime thriller as portrait of complicit love

### Dark Comedy / Absurdism

Films where cruelty and comedy occupy the same frame.

- *The Lobster* (2015, dir. Yorgos Lanthimos) — dystopian satire of romantic obligation
- *The Favourite* (2018, dir. Yorgos Lanthimos) — power as dark comedy
- *The Killing of a Sacred Deer* (2017, dir. Yorgos Lanthimos) — Greek tragedy played deadpan

### Coming-of-Age

Identity formation with genuine stakes and without sentimentality.

- *Eighth Grade* (2018, dir. Bo Burnham) — social anxiety in the smartphone era
- *Waves* (2019, dir. Trey Edward Shults) — Black American family, grief in two movements
- *Minari* (2020, dir. Lee Isaac Chung) — the immigrant dream and its costs

## A24’s Visual Grammar

There is no single A24 look, but recurring visual tendencies:

**Natural light as emotional register.** *Moonlight* uses blue and purple light for emotional cold; amber for warmth and community. *The Witch* uses flat, overcast natural light — no shadows, no comfort.

**The close-up as revelation.** A24 films trust the actor’s face as the primary location of the film’s meaning. Barry Jenkins and Ari Aster both linger on faces past the point of comfort.

**Architecture and setting as psychological extension.** The house in *Hereditary* is a character. The commune in *Midsommar* is designed as a character. Environments externalize interiority.

**Unhurried pacing.** A24 films frequently move slower than genre convention demands. This is intentional — the audience must inhabit the world rather than observe it.

## The A24 Philosophy for Studio Brain

When invoking the A24 voice in Studio Brain:

1. **What is the genre vehicle?** Name the genre being used (horror, teen comedy, sci-fi).
1. **What is the genre hiding?** Name the real emotional or thematic subject beneath the genre surface.
1. **Where is the tonal tension?** What makes it simultaneously funny and devastating, beautiful and horrifying?
1. **What is the film uncomfortable with?** A24 films push into discomfort rather than away from it. What is this scene refusing to look away from?
1. **What does the ending refuse to do?** A24 films rarely provide clean resolution. What does yours leave unresolved — and why is that the honest choice?
