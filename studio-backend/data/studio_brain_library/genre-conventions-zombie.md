# Genre Conventions — Zombie Film

## The Genre's Dominant Traditions

### Romero School (Night/Dawn/Day of the Dead)
- Zombies as social commentary — consumers, soldiers, the underclass
- Siege structure: survivors in a defensible location, turning on each other
- Bleak endings, no redemption arc
- **Influence to draw from:** Group collapse, siege tension, the humans-are-the-real-monsters thesis

### 28 Days Later School
- Rage-infected fast movers (not what this film uses, but note the tonal template)
- Intimate protagonist focus, small cast
- Military/institution as secondary threat
- **Influence to draw from:** Handheld intimacy, the horror of organized human cruelty

### Train to Busan School
- Propulsive, emotional, genre-elevated crowd-pleaser
- Character deaths as gut punches, not just stakes
- Class dynamics embedded in survival access
- **Influence to draw from:** Emotional investment before spectacle, earned sacrifice

### The Walking Dead (TV) School
- Long-form group dynamics, leadership instability
- Moral compromise as ongoing cost
- **What to avoid from this:** Repetitive conflict loops, character regression for plot convenience

### The Road (Film/Novel)
- Dyadic survival — two-person emotional core
- No explanation of the catastrophe
- Love as the only remaining meaningful act
- **Influence to draw from:** Emotional economy, restraint, forward motion as hope

## Tropes to Use Deliberately
- **The concealed bite** — still effective if the concealment is motivated by love, not stupidity
- **The safe place that isn't** — earned if the safety was genuinely believable first
- **Hard choice between survivors** — works when both options are morally defensible

## Tropes to Subvert or Avoid
| Trope | Problem | Alternative |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| Idiot survivor decisions | Breaks audience trust | Make danger unavoidable, not avoidable |
| The lone wolf who's right | Libertarian fantasy | Competence exists within community |
| Women as motivation/victims | Stale | Competence and agency across characters |
| Child as hope symbol | Manipulative if unearned | Children as full characters with fear and agency |
| Military/government as solution | 14 months in — that ship sailed | |

## This Film's Genre Position
Grounded survivor drama with horror elements. Not a horror film with drama elements. The zombie threat is the environment; the human drama is the story.

## Studio Brain Application
When suggesting scene structure or character arcs, filter through this genre position. If a suggestion leans toward action-genre conventions, flag it. Reference specific films from this doc when making tonal comparisons.
