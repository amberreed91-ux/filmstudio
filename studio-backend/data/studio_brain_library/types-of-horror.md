# Types of Horror

A reference guide for Studio Brain — horror’s many forms, mechanics, and the fears they target.

-----

## The Function of Horror

Horror is the only genre whose primary job is to produce a physiological response in the audience. It’s also — at its best — the genre most willing to confront what polite society refuses to look at: mortality, bodily violation, social collapse, the disintegration of self. Horror that only wants to startle is a funhouse. Horror that wants to wound you is art.

> “The oldest and strongest emotion of mankind is fear, and the oldest and strongest kind of fear is fear of the unknown.” — H.P. Lovecraft

-----

## Types of Horror

### Supernatural Horror

**Core fear:** The existence of forces beyond human understanding or control. God’s absence. Something older and more powerful than us.
**Mechanics:** Rules must be established and then violated in ways that feel inevitable in retrospect. The supernatural must feel earned.
**Key films:** *The Exorcist* (1973), *Hereditary* (2018), *The Witch* (2015), *Rosemary’s Baby* (1968), *It Follows* (2014)
**Studio Brain note:** A24’s supernatural horror tends to treat faith, grief, and family as the true monsters — the supernatural element is a manifestation.

-----

### Psychological Horror

**Core fear:** The mind itself is unreliable. Reality is collapsing from the inside. You cannot trust your own perception.
**Mechanics:** Ambiguity is a tool, not a cop-out. The best psychological horror has a coherent internal logic that can be read either way.
**Key films:** *The Shining* (1980), *Black Swan* (2010), *Repulsion* (1965), *Midsommar* (2019), *Enemy* (2013)
**Craft note:** The camera becomes an unreliable narrator. What we see cannot be trusted. Production design and editing do the work.

-----

### Body Horror

**Core fear:** The body as threat, as alien thing, as meat that disobeys. Violation of bodily integrity. Transformation against one’s will.
**Mechanics:** Practical effects over CGI where possible — the physical material reality of bodies makes digital abstraction less effective.
**Key filmmakers:** David Cronenberg, Julia Ducournau, Clive Barker
**Key films:** *The Fly* (1986), *Videodrome* (1983), *Titane* (2021), *Raw* (2016), *Annihilation* (2018)
**Thematic range:** Can address illness, pregnancy, addiction, gender dysphoria, aging — any experience of the body as stranger.

-----

### Slasher

**Core fear:** Being hunted. The randomness of death. Being in the wrong place.
**Mechanics:** A killer with rules (real or perceived). Final girl dynamics. The kill sequence as set piece. Often structured around social transgression as trigger.
**Key filmmakers:** John Carpenter, Wes Craven, James Wan
**Key films:** *Halloween* (1978), *A Nightmare on Elm Street* (1984), *Scream* (1996), *Friday the 13th* (1980)
**Evolution:** *Scream* introduced meta-awareness of slasher rules. *You’re Next* flipped the survival dynamic. *X* (2022) used slasher mechanics to interrogate film history itself.

-----

### Cosmic / Lovecraftian Horror

**Core fear:** Humanity’s total insignificance. The universe is indifferent and incomprehensible. Knowledge itself is dangerous.
**Mechanics:** The entity must never be fully seen or understood — revelation should feel like cognitive collapse, not payoff.
**Key films:** *Annihilation* (2018), *Color Out of Space* (2019), *The Thing* (1982), *Underwater* (2020), *In the Mouth of Madness* (1994)
**Craft note:** Sound design carries more dread than visuals. The score should feel *wrong*.

-----

### Folk Horror

**Core fear:** Community as threat. The land itself as hostile. Old systems of belief that demand blood.
**Mechanics:** Isolation. The protagonists are outsiders who don’t understand the rules until too late. Ritual as climax.
**Key films:** *The Wicker Man* (1973), *Midsommar* (2019), *The Witch* (2015), *Children of the Corn* (1984), *Apostle* (2018)
**Thematic signature:** Religion, sacrifice, the pastoral idyll as nightmare, pagan vs. Christian tension.

-----

### Found Footage

**Core fear:** Authenticity. Real footage of real events. You are watching evidence.
**Mechanics:** Handheld camera, limited visibility, audio as tension builder, the camera as both documentation and limitation. The frame edge is where danger lives.
**Key films:** *The Blair Witch Project* (1999), *Cloverfield* (2008), *REC* (2007), *Paranormal Activity* (2007), *Lake Mungo* (2008)
**Craft note:** *Lake Mungo* uses the found footage framework as grief documentary — the horror is emotional, not spectacle.

-----

### Haunted House / Gothic Horror

**Core fear:** Space with memory. The house as externalized psyche. The past refusing to stay dead.
**Mechanics:** Architecture is character. Room reveals. The door that shouldn’t open. The history of a structure as plot.
**Key films:** *The Haunting of Hill House* (film: 1963, series: 2018), *Rebecca* (1940), *The Others* (2001), *Crimson Peak* (2015), *The Innocents* (1961)
**Craft note:** Gothic horror often uses horror to examine grief, repression, and the weight of family history.

-----

### Zombie / Infection Horror

**Core fear:** Dehumanization. The people you love becoming threats. Societal collapse. The uncontrollable spread of catastrophe.
**Mechanics:** The zombies are rarely the real horror — the human survivors are. Infection horror is a vehicle for social collapse narratives.
**Key films:** *Night of the Living Dead* (1968), *28 Days Later* (2002), *Train to Busan* (2016), *World War Z* (2013)
**Romero’s legacy:** Every zombie film is in conversation with Romero’s racial and class politics. The genre began as social critique.

-----

### Social Horror

**Core fear:** Systems, institutions, and social structures designed to oppress. The horror of being seen as less than human by the powerful.
**Mechanics:** Blurs horror with social realism. The supernatural element is a literalization of real social violence.
**Key filmmakers:** Jordan Peele, Get Out’s DNA has spread across a generation of horror films.
**Key films:** *Get Out* (2017), *Us* (2019), *Sorry to Bother You* (2018), *His House* (2020), *Nope* (2022)

-----

### Survival Horror

**Core fear:** Death by environment. Nature, circumstance, or system as unstoppable force. The body’s limits.
**Mechanics:** Resource scarcity. Incremental deterioration. Time pressure. Geography as antagonist.
**Key films:** *The Descent* (2005), *127 Hours* (2010), *A Quiet Place* (2018), *Bone Tomahawk* (2015), *No Exit* (2022)

-----

## Horror’s Emotional Architecture

### Dread vs. Terror vs. Horror

- **Dread:** What happens *before* — anticipation, the feeling that something is wrong. The most potent state.
- **Terror:** The moment of confrontation — the reveal, the jump.
- **Horror:** What remains *after* — the lingering wrongness that a great horror film leaves in the body.

The jump scare lives in terror. The great horror films live in dread and aftermath.

### The Rule of Obscuration

Show less than you think you need to. The audience’s imagination is always more powerful than any production design.

-----

## Using Horror in Studio Brain

When generating horror scenes, specify:

- **What fear type?** Physical, existential, psychological, social
- **What is the horror a metaphor for?** The best horror answers this clearly
- **Dread or terror?** The desired emotional register shapes pacing, lighting, sound design
- **What does the audience know that the character doesn’t?** Dramatic irony is horror’s most reliable engine
